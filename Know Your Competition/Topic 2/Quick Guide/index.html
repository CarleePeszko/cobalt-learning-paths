Question 1 — Where you stand today
Your share, revenue, units, and ASP right now answer where you are positioned relative to competitors and the market.
Question 2 — Where you're going
The direction your numbers are moving relative to the category tells you whether you're gaining or losing share — and lands you in one of these three patterns.
You Category
Gaining share
What you're seeing
Your revenue and/or units growing faster than the category.
What it means
You're outpacing the market — competitors are losing ground to you.
You Category
Watch
What you're seeing
Revenue looks okay but units are declining, or you're growing slower than the category.
What it means
Surface numbers can hide a share loss. Dig into unit trends and compare to category growth rate.
You Category
Losing share
What you're seeing
Revenue or units declining while the category grows, or share % falling over time.
What it means
Competitors are capturing growth you're not. The pattern worth investigating in Topic 3.

Category Performance
Quick pulse check
Data sourceCategory Performance
·
Lag~3 weeks
·
Date rangeCustom or presets
Use this when
You want a fast read on share against the full subcategory — every ASIN, broader denominator than your segment. The Home page has a Market Share card for top categories at a glance, but Category Performance is where the useful work happens.
How to find it
Primary
Left-hand navigation → Category Performance → select your subcategory
If you don't know your subcategory
From the Home page, open the Sales Breakdown table and click any row — Category Performance opens pre-filtered to that subcategory.
1
Switch to Benchmark Mode & filter to your brand
Toggle Benchmark Mode, then filter to your brand. You'll see your revenue, units, and ASP vs. the subcategory average side by side — not against named competitors, but against the aggregate.
2
Navigate the category tree
Move up and down the hierarchy to see your share at different scopes — your subcategory vs. parent vs. broader family. Watch whether share is growing or declining at each level.
What you'll find
Three things to read in Benchmark Mode, in order:
1 Your three metrics vs. subcategory average— revenue, units, ASP side by side
2 Trend over time— whether you're keeping pace with category growth
3 Share at parent & broader levels— signals adjacent opportunity or shrinking relevance

The Marketplace dropdown sits at the top, followed by the Brand selector and subcategory tree. Subcategories with a circle are selectable; arrow icons expand the tree to deeper levels. Pick one leaf-node subcategory per report.

Reading your numbers
Read together, not in isolation
A reading lens for any of the methods above
Whichever method you used, you'll see these metrics. Market share is the headline — it tells you where you stand. Revenue, units, and ASP are the inputs that explain why share moved. Below is BIC's actual position in Men's Disposable Shaving Razors over the last 365 days.
BIC Market Share
8.1%
Absolute -1.4 pp
Relative -14.4%
Two ways to describe the same drop. -1.4 pp is the absolute change — the gap between this period's share and last period's. -14.4% is the relative change — how much BIC's share shrank as a fraction of its former size. Both are correct. Cobalt shows the absolute change in the Brands table; the relative change appears in the brand slide-out view.
Revenue
How much is BIC generating?
$3.75M
-3.3% YoY (-$128.9K)
Read together with the segment BIC revenue declined 3.3% while the segment grew +12.9%. BIC is losing ground in a growing market — the Losing share pattern.
Units Sold
How much demand is BIC capturing?
311,438
YoY change not shown by default
Read together with revenue and ASP Units tell you the volume side of the story. To see YoY unit change, set the date range to the prior period and compare manually.
ASP
How is BIC competing on price?
$12.04
vs. $11.60 market average
Read together with units BIC's ASP is $0.44 above the market — a premium position. With share declining, is the premium protecting margin or pricing BIC out?
Quick wins
1
Know your share from all three sources. Pull the same date range across Market Analysis, Category Performance, and AI Analyst Search Insights. They'll be different — and that's expected.
2
Apply the metric combination signals. Match your revenue / units / ASP pattern against the gain / watch / loss signals above. If you're in a Watch or Risk pattern, surface it to your team.

Why numbers differ across features

Same subcategory, different numbers. Each view answers a different question about a different version of the market — none of them are wrong.
