Section 1

How to read the data: revenue, units, and ASP

Cobalt surfaces three core metrics for benchmarking. Each answers a different question — and the real insight comes from reading them together, not in isolation.

Revenue
How much is this brand generating?

Total sales dollars. Tells you scale and overall position, but not efficiency or demand. Revenue can grow while share shrinks if the market grows faster.

Units Sold
How much demand is this brand capturing?

Volume of products sold — the demand signal, independent of price. A brand losing units while holding revenue is raising prices to compensate.

ASP
How is this brand competing on price?

Average selling price. Reveals pricing strategy and market position. A rising ASP with declining units suggests trading volume for margin.

Three signals to watch for

As you look at these metrics across Cobalt features, you're watching for one of three situations. A detailed reference table with all metric combinations is in Section 6 — once you've seen your own numbers, that table will make more sense.

Signal What you're seeing What it means
Gaining share Your revenue and/or units growing faster than the category You're outpacing the market — competitors are losing ground to you
Watch Revenue looks okay but units are declining, or you're growing slower than the category Surface-level numbers can hide a share loss — dig into unit trends and compare to category growth rate
Losing share Revenue or units declining while the category grows, or share % falling over time Competitors are capturing growth you're not — this is the pattern worth investigating in Topic 3
Trend matters more than a single number

Look at 3–6 months before drawing conclusions. Growing revenue in a fast-growing market can still mean losing share. One slow month is noise; six consecutive months of declining unit share is a signal.

Section 3
Market Analysis

Your segment view

Market Analysis is where your segments live — and where you'll do your deepest benchmarking work. Because your segment was built with specific filters (brands, keywords, minimum review counts), the data here reflects your actual competitive set, not every product Amazon has in that subcategory.

This precision matters. The Men's Disposable Razors subcategory includes hundreds of ASINs at wildly different price points, review counts, and quality levels — many of which are not competing for the same shopper you are. Your segment, filtered to the brands and product types you actually compete with, gives you a benchmark that means something.

The six tabs in Market Analysis follow a natural analysis progression that moves from market-level to root cause: start with the Overview to understand where you stand and what the market looks like. Move to Brands to see which specific brands are causing the share shifts you observed. Go to Products to see which products from those brands are doing the work. Use Categories if your segment spans multiple subcategories. Then Attributes and Pricing start revealing what those products have in common — the features, configurations, and price points that are connecting with buyers. You don't need all six every time, but this is the logical path from "what's happening?" to "why is it happening?"

In Topic 1, we built four segments at different levels of specificity: Shaving Razors (broadest) → Disposable Razors → and then two at the same level: Women's Disposable and Men's Disposable. For benchmarking your competitive position in Men's Disposable Razors specifically, you'd use the Men's Disposable segment. The broader parent segments are useful for category context but show a larger pool than your true competitive set for that specific subcategory.

Find your segment

In the left nav, click Market Analysis. The segment list has two tabs: Saved market segments (all segments in your organization that you have access to) and Created by me (only segments you created). If your organization has many segments, the easiest approach is to go to the Created by me tab and use the search field to find the segment you want, or use the search bar directly from the main list. For this guide, open your Men's Disposable Shaving Razors segment.

Date range note: Market Analysis only offers preset date ranges — no custom date entry. "Last 90 days" in Market Analysis resolves to the last 90 days of available segment data, on roughly a 1-week lag. This will not match "Last 90 days" in Category Performance or AI Analyst exactly, since each feature's date presets anchor to different data update schedules. For the closest comparison, use the same preset across features and note that the actual windows may differ by a few weeks. See the Appendix for more on data lag by feature.

Once you're in your segment, Market Analysis organizes data across six tabs. Here's what each one is for and when to use it.

Overview tab
Primary
Use this when: you want your top-line market share and a quick read on how all brands in your segment compare

The Overview tab is your starting point. It shows your brand's share of the total segment — not the full subcategory, but the competitive set you defined. This is the number that answers "where do I stand among my actual competitors?"

At the top of the page, your market share percentage is displayed prominently alongside total segment revenue for the period. This share is calculated only across the ASINs in your segment — the brands and products that met your filter criteria when you built it. This is your share of your actual competitive set, not the full subcategory.

Below the headline share number, the Segment Composition brand comparison table lets you switch between three views using the metric selector:

Revenue view

Shows total revenue for each brand in the segment for the selected period. Use this to understand the revenue gap between you and your competitors — who's generating more, who's generating less, and by how much.

Market Share view

Shows each brand's share of the segment as a percentage. This is the most direct competitive comparison — you can see at a glance who owns the most share and how yours compares. Track this number over time; consistent share gain or loss is your most important competitive signal.

Units view

Shows total units sold per brand. Compare units alongside revenue to understand whether competitors are growing through volume or pricing. A brand growing revenue without growing units is raising prices; a brand growing units without growing revenue is likely discounting.

Average Selling Price (ASP)

The ASP view shows each brand's average selling price for the period. Use this to understand where you sit in the price stack relative to your competitors. A significant gap between your ASP and a growing competitor's ASP tells you something about how they're competing — volume at lower prices, or premium positioning at higher prices.

🪒 Razor example — Overview tab

In the Men's Disposable Razors segment (YTD, April 30, 2026), BIC's share of the segment is 8.1% with total segment revenue of $15.5M. The segment includes all brands that met your filter criteria — you'll see the top brands most prominently in charts and tables, with others appearing further down or grouped in an "other" category for visualization purposes. This 8.1% is meaningfully different from BIC's share of the full Men's Disposable Shaving Razors subcategory — because the denominator here is your filtered competitive set, not every ASIN Amazon has categorized in that subcategory including inactive listings and low-quality products. In the revenue view, you can see the relative scale of each brand; in the units view, you can start to see whether any brand's growth is volume-driven or price-driven.

Brands tab
Primary
Use this when: you want to see which brands are gaining or losing share over time and when those shifts happened

The Brands tab gives you the time-series view of your competitive set. While the Overview tab shows a snapshot for the selected period, the Brands tab lets you scroll through a stacked area chart to see how each brand's share has moved month by month. As one brand's band grows, another shrinks — making it easy to see which specific brands are responsible for share movement in the market.

Set the chart to Market Share using the metric selector in the top right. The stacked area chart shows each brand as a colored band. You can also switch to Revenue or Units to see those trend lines. Hover over any point to see exact figures for that month.

This tab is where you start connecting market-level trends to brand-level causes. If the Overview shows the market grew 15% but your share dropped, the Brands tab tells you which brand captured that growth and approximately when it started happening.

🪒 Razor example — Brands tab

In the Men's Disposable Razors segment YTD, the Brands tab shows BIC's share growing slightly at the start of the year before leveling off — not a dramatic decline, but also not sustained growth. This tells a more nuanced story than a single snapshot: BIC had some early momentum but has not continued to build on it. Meanwhile, hovering over any point on the chart shows the exact share for each brand at that moment in time, making it easy to pinpoint when specific shifts happened. The trend view here is more valuable than any single data point — a short-term gain that flattens is a different signal than consistent growth, and the Brands tab makes that distinction visible.

Products tab
Secondary
Use this when: you want to see which specific products are driving the brand-level trends you spotted in the Brands tab

Once you've identified which brands are gaining or losing share, the Products tab lets you go one level deeper — which products from those brands are actually making the moves. The stacked area chart shows the top 5, 10, or 55 products by revenue, each as a colored band. Switch between Revenue, Units, and Market Share to understand whether a brand's growth is coming from one breakout product or distributed across their catalog.

This is where you start answering the "how are they doing it?" question — before you can understand strategy (pricing, keywords, assortment), you need to know which products are responsible.

🪒 Razor example — Products tab

If the Brands tab showed Kitsch gaining share from BIC, the Products tab tells you whether that's coming from a single Kitsch product that's breaking out or from broad growth across their catalog. If it's one product — a specific pack size or style — that's a very different signal than if Kitsch is growing evenly across everything they sell. That distinction shapes what you look at next.

Categories tab
Reference
Use this when: your segment spans multiple subcategories and you want to see how revenue breaks down across them

The Categories tab shows revenue broken down by category node for the products in your segment. It's most useful when you're working with a broader parent segment that spans multiple subcategories — for example, the Shaving Razors segment that includes disposable, cartridge, and other subcategory types. You can see which subcategory is driving the most revenue and how that has shifted over time.

Note: the Men's Disposable Razors segment is limited to one subcategory, so the Categories tab doesn't show a meaningful breakdown there. The screenshot below uses the broader Shaving Razors segment to illustrate what this tab looks like when your segment spans multiple subcategories. If your segment covers multiple subcategories, this tab is a useful way to see how revenue is distributed across them.

Attributes tab
Reference
Use this when: you want to understand what product characteristics are driving revenue within your segment — color, size, material, and more

The Attributes tab shows how different product attributes map to revenue within your segment. Available attributes depend on what data Cobalt has for the products in your segment — currently this includes attributes like color, size, and material. Each attribute type shows a bar chart breaking down revenue and units by that attribute value.

The specific attributes available will vary by segment and category. For some segments, attributes like color or material are very revealing — for others, the breakdown may be less directly actionable. The value of this tab is highly dependent on your product category and what attributes your customers use to choose between products.

Pricing tab
Primary
Use this when: you want to understand where your revenue sits in the price stack and how your ASP compares to competitors over time

The Pricing tab surfaces several views for understanding how pricing maps to performance within your segment. ASP movements explain a lot of what you see in revenue and unit numbers — a brand losing units but growing revenue is almost always raising price, and the Pricing tab confirms it with the data.

Revenue and products by price band

The first view on the Pricing tab shows how revenue and products are distributed across price bands within your segment. Use this to understand where the segment's revenue is concentrated by price tier and how product count maps to revenue in each band.

Average selling price trend and revenue migration

These two charts show together: the ASP trend over time for your brand vs. the segment average, and how revenue has migrated between price bands over the period. The ASP trend tells you whether the gap between your price and the segment average is widening or narrowing. Revenue migration shows whether your customers — and the segment overall — are moving up-market or down-market.

Your Brand Revenue by Price Band

This view breaks your revenue into price tiers and shows how much of your revenue comes from each band, alongside your share within that band. It reveals whether your revenue is concentrated in growing or declining price tiers and how your distribution compares to the broader segment.

Price Band Analysis table

The table view gives you the full breakdown by price band in a structured format — your revenue, units, ASP, and share within each band, alongside segment totals. Use this for a precise read on where you're over- or under-indexed vs. the market.

🪒 Razor example — Pricing tab

In the Men's Disposable Razors segment YTD, the Pricing tab would show BIC's revenue concentration by price tier and whether BIC's ASP trend is moving above or below the segment average. If BIC's ASP is rising while the segment average holds flat, that confirms BIC is moving up-market — which explains the unit share story visible in the Overview and Brands tabs. The Price Band Analysis table makes it precise: which specific price tier is BIC's revenue concentrated in, and is that tier growing or contracting in the segment overall?

Putting Market Analysis together

The six tabs follow a natural analysis progression: Overview tells you where the market stands and where you sit within it. Brands shows which brands are causing the share shifts you saw in Overview. Products reveals which specific products from those brands are doing the work. Categories helps if your segment spans multiple subcategories. Attributes starts to show the product-level patterns — what configurations are winning. Pricing connects it back to the revenue and ASP story. You don't need to work through all six every time — start with Overview and Brands to answer the core benchmarking questions, then go deeper in Products and Pricing when you've identified something worth investigating. Topic 3 is where you take that investigation further: who's disrupting the category, how, and what it means for you.

Section 4
Home page & Category Performance

Your category-level view

The Home page and Category Performance use Category Performance data — every ASIN in the subcategory — so numbers will differ from your segment. These views give you a fast read on your position against the full market and are useful for a quick share check or understanding your brand in the context of the broader category landscape.

Home page — Market Share card and Sales Breakdown table

The Market Share card at the top of the Home page shows your share across your top 5 categories, using your brand filter and date range selection. Use the filters at the top of the page (Country, Category, Brand) to focus the data — especially if you have multiple brands integrated.

Important: the Market Share card uses the date range selected at the top of the page, which is on a 72-hour lag from Seller/Vendor Central. The numbers here are a quick read, but for a more accurate market share comparison, go to the Sales Breakdown by Category table below it.

The Sales Breakdown by Category table normalizes the date range to account for the Category Performance data source being on a 3-week lag — so the date range shown at the bottom right of the table will be different from the rest of the page. This is intentional: it means you're comparing your sales data and category performance data for the same actual window, giving you a more accurate share figure. Note the date range label in the bottom right of the table.

Expand any category row to see your share broken down by subcategory. For the razor example, you can drill into Women's Disposable and Men's Disposable separately to see your share at each level.

Category Performance — Benchmark Mode

Navigate to Category Performance in the left nav and select your subcategory. You can also get there directly by clicking on any subcategory from the Home page Sales Breakdown table — it will open Category Performance pre-filtered to that subcategory.

In Benchmark Mode, filter to your brand using the brand selector at the top. You'll see your brand's revenue, units, and ASP compared to the subcategory average side by side — not against individual named competitors, but against the aggregate. Benchmark Mode is useful for understanding whether you're above or below the category's growth rate on each metric. Date ranges can be changed to standard presets or custom ranges.

Navigating the category tree

You can move up and down the category tree to see your share at different levels of the Amazon category hierarchy. This is a useful way to understand your brand's position at different scopes — not just within the specific subcategory you sell in, but within the broader category family.

Pay attention to how your share and trend numbers change as you navigate. Going up several levels — for example from Men's Disposable Shaving Razors up to Shaving & Hair Removal Products — will dramatically change your share percentage, because the denominator now includes a much wider range of products, including product types where you may have no presence at all. A lower share at the parent category level isn't necessarily bad; it reflects how concentrated your brand is in specific subcategories. The more useful signal is whether your share at each level is growing or declining over time.

How to use this: If you see your share at the parent category level growing faster than at the subcategory level, it may indicate your brand is expanding into adjacent areas. If share is declining at the parent level while holding at the subcategory, you're likely holding your ground in your core subcategory but losing relevance in adjacent spaces. Use this to identify where to focus — and where there may be untapped adjacent opportunity.

Date range note: Category Performance data is on a 3-week lag. The date ranges can be set to standard presets or custom ranges, but the data available will reflect that lag. For cross-feature comparison with Market Analysis (1-week lag) or AI Analyst (fixed last 365 days), note that the same date range selected across features will not reflect the exact same data window.
🪒 Razor example — Home page and Category Performance

In Category Performance Benchmark Mode filtered to BIC at the Men's Disposable Shaving Razors subcategory level, you'd see BIC's revenue, units, and ASP compared to the subcategory average — a different view than the segment, because this includes all ASINs, not just the ones in your segment. Navigating up to Shaving & Hair Removal Products would show BIC's share of the entire shaving category, which will be dramatically lower — BIC's Men's Disposable Razor products now represent a small slice of the full shaving market. This isn't concerning in isolation, but tracking whether that broader share is growing or declining over time tells you if BIC is gaining or losing relevance in the category family overall.

When to use this vs. Market Analysis

Use the Home page for a weekly pulse check — your share at a glance. Use Category Performance Benchmark Mode when you want to compare your metrics to the full category average or navigate the category tree. Use Market Analysis when you need to understand exactly how you compare to the specific brands you compete with.

Section 5
AI Analyst

Your automated benchmark report

AI Analyst generates structured, presentation-ready reports from Cobalt data. Select your brand and a subcategory, and AI Analyst pulls from multiple data sources, runs the analysis, and delivers a complete report with written insights, data visualizations, and strategic takeaways — ready to share with your team. No manual data pulling, no spreadsheet assembly.

Keep your brand context in mind

AI Analyst surfaces data-driven takeaways based on performance metrics, but it doesn't know your brand's strategy, positioning, or internal goals. A report might flag declining share in a price band, but if your brand intentionally focuses on premium positioning or margin optimization, that context changes how you'd interpret the finding. Use the report as a starting point and layer in your own knowledge of your business.

How to run a Benchmark Brand report

1

Navigate to AI Analyst

In the left nav, click AI Analyst (near the top of the menu). Click the Benchmark Brand button to start a new report.

2

Configure your report

Select your Marketplace (US and Germany available now), your Brand from the dropdown (only your integrated brands appear), and browse or search the category tree to select your Subcategory. Reports can only be run on the lowest-level subcategories — you'll see a circle next to selectable subcategories. If you see an arrow, expand it to find deeper levels.

3

Run Analysis

Click Run Analysis. Reports generate quickly, often in under a minute. If you navigate away while it's generating, return to the AI Analyst main page and refresh to see your completed report.

What the Benchmark Brand report covers

All Benchmark Brand reports cover the last 365 days of data. There is currently no way to change the date range — all reports use this fixed window. Your brand's metrics use first-party account data from your Amazon integrations; competitor and category data uses Jungle Scout estimates.

Executive Summary

Your brand's revenue with a trend sparkline, current market share with share point change, and three strategic takeaways. Start here for a quick read on your brand's overall position before reading the detailed sections.

Brand Performance

Your revenue, units, and ASP compared to the subcategory. A dual-axis chart shows your market share trend alongside subcategory revenue over time. Use this to see whether you're keeping pace with, outperforming, or falling behind the subcategory overall.

Competitive Landscape

Top competing brands ranked by market share with share movement, plus a revenue-vs-ASP bubble chart. Shows who's gaining ground and how their pricing compares to yours. Use this to identify which brands are taking share and how their price positioning differs from your own.

Price Positioning

Revenue distribution across price bands comparing your brand to the subcategory. Key findings scored as Opportunity (bands where you're underrepresented but the subcategory is growing) or Risk (where you're concentrated but losing share). Share-per-band donut charts show your position in each tier. Use this to evaluate whether your pricing aligns with where demand is heading.

Search Insights

Your brand's search visibility across the subcategory, measured by Share of Voice (SOV), Keyword Coverage, and Top Competitor SOV. This is one of the most important benchmarking signals available — most Amazon purchases start with a search, and everyone is competing for their share of the digital shelf. You don't need to be tracking keywords elsewhere in Cobalt to see this data; keywords are identified automatically based on the top-selling products in the subcategory.

Search Insights: what the metrics mean

Share of Voice (SOV) — how visible your brand is across the subcategory's most important keywords, weighted by search volume. A higher SOV means your products appear more prominently for the keywords shoppers use most.

Keyword Coverage — the percentage of the top 1,000 keywords where at least one of your products appears in the first 3 pages of results. High coverage with low SOV means you show up broadly but aren't ranking near the top.

Top Competitor SOV — the highest SOV held by any single brand. The gap between yours and theirs tells you how much visibility you'd need to gain to lead in search.

Keywords are grouped into four action categories: Attack (competitors lead, you have a small presence — strong opportunities to grow), Expand (you're present but underweight), Defend (you lead but could lose ground), and No Presence (keywords you don't appear on at all).

Limitations to know

  • Last 365 days, fixed — the date range cannot be changed. Every Benchmark Brand report covers the same window. To compare to Market Analysis or Category Performance data, set those features to a 365-day range for the closest match.
  • One subcategory at a time — reports are scoped to a single leaf-node subcategory. For analysis across multiple subcategories, Market Analysis segments may be a better fit.
  • Category Performance data for competitors — your own brand metrics use first-party integration data; competitor and category data uses Jungle Scout estimates. Numbers will differ from Market Analysis.
  • No ASIN-level detail — brand-level only. You can see that a competitor grew share but not which of their products drove it.
  • No export yet — reports are view-only in-app. Use screenshots for sharing. Export and PDF options are in development.
  • Beta access — during beta, your organization has access to one L2 subcategory and all lower-level subcategories beneath it. Reach out to your CSM to change your assigned L2.
🪒 Razor example — AI Analyst Benchmark Brand

The BIC Men's Disposable Shaving Razors Benchmark Report (covering the last 365 days) surfaces this picture: BIC revenue $2.85M (down 4.72%), market share 6.1% (down 1.35pp). The Executive Summary flags three key issues — severe volume loss despite pricing power (28.2% unit decline vs. 14.3% category unit growth), share erosion across all active price tiers, and a wide search presence with a persistent SOV gap. In Price Positioning, BIC is over-concentrated in the $10–$16 tier (37% of revenue) while the $7.50–$10 tier is growing 141.6% and BIC holds only 10.4% revenue share there — flagged as an Opportunity. Search Insights shows BIC ranks #3 in SOV at 8.1%, with 75.6% keyword coverage, but trails category leader Gillette's 22.9% SOV by 14.9pp. Together, these sections give you the orientation — share loss driven by volume decline, price band misalignment, and a search visibility gap that's widening vs. the category leader. The next step is Market Analysis to understand which specific competitors are taking which share, and Topic 3 to understand how.

How to get the most out of it

Use AI Analyst Benchmark Brand to get oriented and identify the questions worth investigating. Then bring those questions into Market Analysis. The report tells you something is happening; your segment helps you understand why and against whom.

Section 6

Side-by-side: the razor example across methods

The same question — how is BIC performing in Men's Disposable Razors? — produces different answers depending on where you look, because the data sources and time windows are different. Here's what each method tells you at a glance.

A note on date ranges in this example: The AI Analyst Benchmark Brand report always covers the last 365 days and cannot be changed. The Market Analysis and Category Performance examples in this guide use a YTD view (through April 30, 2026). For a true apples-to-apples comparison across all three methods, set Market Analysis and Category Performance to a 365-day range. The numbers below will differ partly because the time windows don't match — that's expected, not an error. The interpretation framework is the same regardless of which window you use.

Category Performance / AI Analyst

Full subcategory · All ASINs · AI Analyst: last 365 days

✅ Tells you: BIC's position and trend vs. the entire subcategory — share %, revenue, units, ASP, price band concentration, and search visibility.

❌ Doesn't tell you: How BIC performs against specific competitors vying for the same buyer. No filtering to a true competitive set.

➡️ Next action: Use price band and search SOV findings to identify where to investigate further — then go to Market Analysis.

Market Analysis (Segment)

Custom competitive set · Filtered ASINs · YTD in this example

✅ Tells you: BIC's share within the brands you're actually competing with for a sale — a more precise benchmark. Trend view shows when brand-level shifts started.

❌ Doesn't tell you: BIC's position in the full subcategory picture — for that, Category Performance and AI Analyst give the broader context.

➡️ Next action: Identify which brand is gaining share fastest and how — adding SKUs, expanding price points, growing a breakout product, accumulating reviews? That sets up Topic 3.

Note on date ranges and AI Analyst

AI Analyst Benchmark Brand always covers the last 365 days — you can't change the time window. Market Analysis and Category Performance let you choose your own date range. If you want to compare numbers directly across all three features, set Market Analysis and Category Performance to a 365-day range. If you're only using one or two features, use whatever date range makes sense for your analysis — there's no need to normalize unless you're doing a cross-feature comparison.

Appendix

Why numbers differ across features

If the same subcategory shows different numbers in different Cobalt features, that's expected. The features use different data sources, different ASIN pools, and different date windows — each designed for a different level of precision and purpose.

Category Performance data
Used by: Home page, Category Performance, AI Analyst (category/competitor data)
  • Every ASIN in the subcategory
  • Includes inactive, low-volume, and miscategorized products
  • Broad view of the full subcategory
  • ~3-week data lag
  • Date range: adjustable in Category Performance (standard presets or custom); fixed at last 365 days in AI Analyst
Segment data
Used by: Market Analysis
  • Your filtered ASIN pool — not a raw category pull
  • Excludes products that don't meet your filter criteria
  • Uses a different sales estimation model than Category Performance
  • ~1-week data lag
  • Date range: adjustable via standard presets
  • More precise; customizable to variant and attribute level
First-party account data
Used by: AI Analyst (your brand metrics only)
  • Your own Seller Central or Vendor Central data via Amazon integration
  • Only your integrated brands appear in the brand dropdown
  • Used for your brand's revenue, units, and ASP in Benchmark Brand
  • Competitor and category data still uses Category Performance estimates

Numbers will always differ between these sources for the same subcategory. Neither is "wrong" — they're answering different questions about different versions of the market. The gap is larger when your segment has tight filters; smaller when it's broad.
